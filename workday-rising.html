01

Need

Buyers at Rising needed to try the workflow, not hear about it

Context

Rising is a marketing event. The demo still has to be real.

Workday Rising is the annual customer conference — booths, scheduled meetings, buyers walking up with their own buying process. Healthcare and public-sector teams already have preferred catalog items, off-contract exceptions, and policy they cannot violate in a demo. If the prototype ignores that, the conversation dies.

I scoped two moments that kept coming up on the floor: “show me the laptops I’m allowed to buy” and “this isn’t in the catalog — now what?” Those are the same catalog / non-catalog split in Intake Agent, tested with real operators before the product fully caught up.

  1. 01

    Hear the constraint

    Buyers named the failure: too many results in chat, or a jump to a form they didn’t trust.

  2. 02

    Pull the system

    I reused design-system patterns — catalog rows, policy chips, side panel — instead of inventing a demo-only UI.

  3. 03

    Build in Cursor

    Interactive HTML, not a video. They could click overflow, scan policy labels, and fill a structured request.

  4. 04

    Translate back

    I wrote what had to be true in the product: keep the thread focused, reuse the catalog, capture required fields in-panel.

Why HTML, not Figma

The demo had to survive a live objection

I built these in Cursor against design-system patterns so a buyer could interrupt, click a different path, and still see policy labels and a real next step. A static file cannot do that. The same design-to-code habit shows up in Mobile Inventory — Figma specs into production PRs.
