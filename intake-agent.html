01

Problem

Too many doors — no single place to get a verified answer

Typical employee path today

Search Confluence Ask on Slack Open a purchase form Wrong guidance Compliance risk Escalate to manager ✦

Employees asked the same questions — “How do I buy X?” and “Is this allowed?” — over and over. Answers lived in wikis, Slack threads, and forms: slow to find, easy to get wrong, and they wanted the source before acting.

  • VolumeInternal support said half their week was repeat “how do I…?” questions
  • FreshnessWritten guides go out of date; training everyone again doesn’t scale
  • StakesWrong buying guidance has real compliance and financial consequences
02

Exploration

What we tried, what we ruled out, and what held up

Form-first · rejected

Failed due to guess-first routing

Structured forms worked when users already knew what they needed. For everyone else, step one was a guess — not a viable entry point.

  • New hiresDidn’t know which category to pick
  • PolicyNo way to ask “is this allowed?” before committing
  • RigiditySame form structure regardless of complexity

Pure chat · rejected

Failed due to no workflow handoff

Requisition is a governed workflow — not a chatbot brief. Chat-style surfaces couldn’t keep answers in policy or hand off the next real step.

  • ContextPolicy, catalog, and approvals — not one-off Q&A
  • RoutingWeak ties to sources and deterministic routing
  • TrustJargon and tool knowledge before useful help

Hybrid entry · shipped

One entry point for repeat tasks, open search, and policy-heavy questions — with sources and a real next step.

  • ShortcutsAdmin-curated Quick Links for repeat tasks
  • SearchRole-tuned suggestions on first load
  • AssistantGrounded replies when context matters
03

Solution

Catalog, non-catalog, and the admin loop that keeps both safe

Catalog
Canvas inside the chat.

The Workday design kept catalog in conversation — a reply, a card, a button. Sana introduces a catalog canvas into agent chat so people can search, compare, and add in one place — faster, with less back-and-forth.

  1. Prev · Workday · Chat
  2. Now · Sana · Canvas
Non-catalog
A modal instead of answering in the composer.

Workday collected non-catalog details through chat — the agent asked in the thread, people typed answers in the input box. Sana moves that into a structured input modal: one question at a time, then a complete request.

  1. Prev · Chat · Input
  2. Now · Sana · Modal
Admin
Upload policy. Approve the path.

Workday admins uploaded a policy document, then typed the purchase path by hand. In Sana, the same upload drafts a compliance purchase path — admins approve it and adjust the details.

  1. Prev · Workday · Manual
  2. Now · Sana · Generated
04

Outcomes

What we’d do differently next time

“If the rules don’t match real behavior, the ‘AI’ part doesn’t matter—the experience breaks.”

Push for a live admin preview earlier. The sandbox helped, but seeing the full user-facing experience before launch would have caught more rule gaps before employees hit them.
